Bow Playstyles

Four Ways to Loose an Arrow

The Bow rewards constant repositioning, focus mode, and stamina management over raw combos — which means there isn't one "correct" way to play it. Below are the four playstyles we see the most in the field, ranked by how much they ask of you. Pick the one that matches your pace, or steal a bit from each once you've got the fundamentals down.

Playstyle 01

Dash Dancing

★★★★☆

Range: Short–Mid Focus: Repositioning

Built around Dash Dancing's dodge-and-fire rhythm — you're rarely standing still. Circle the target, slot in charged shots between steps, and let Shooting Booster keep your uptime honest even when you're spending half the fight sidestepping tail swipes.

Shooting Booster HFB Coating

Playstyle 02

Dragonpiercer Sniping

★★★★★

Range: Long Focus: Precision burst

The patient build. You give up sustained pressure for one shot that actually matters — tracking a weak point, holding position, and cashing it in the moment the window opens. Punishing to learn, but nothing else in the kit hits this hard from this far away.

Power Coating Weak-Point Timing

Playstyle 03

8-Gauge Bow

★★☆☆☆

Range: Short Focus: Sustained pressure

The easiest on-ramp into ranged play. Get in close, spam your spread shots, and trade a bit of safety for near-constant uptime. Less flashy than the other three, but forgiving enough that it's usually where newer bow users start before branching out.

Spread Shot Close Quarters

Playstyle 04

Hybrid

★★★★★

Range: All-range Focus: Adaptive

Dash Dancing footwork with Dragonpiercer timing, swapped mid-hunt depending on the matchup. Nothing about Hybrid is beginner-friendly — it asks you to already be comfortable with the other three before it starts paying off — but it's the closest thing the Bow has to a "does everything" answer.
